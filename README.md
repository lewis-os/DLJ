# DLJ — Danika Lewis Journal

> A public journal of ideas worth understanding today and remembering tomorrow.

DLJ is Danika Lewis's journal of selected posts, emerging ideas, and human questions. It is not a transcript of private conversations, a formal research project, or a news publication. Each entry begins with something Danika chose to stop and think about, then makes it understandable for a reader encountering the subject for the first time.

Each entry preserves the public source as faithfully as the available record permits, distinguishes the source's claims from established facts, and then moves through Danika's reflection, the human stakes, unanswered questions, and a **Letter from 2026**. The aim is not to predict perfectly. It is to make a difficult idea clear, record why it mattered, and leave enough context for a future reader—or listener—to understand the moment.

The entries are deliberately structured so they can later support a NotebookLM conversation, podcast, narrated video, or other adaptation without losing source boundaries or human context.

## How DLJ is organized

```text
├── README.md
├── TIMELINE.md
├── entries/

│       └── 2026-07-26-supernova-os.md
├── reflections/
│   └── weekly/
│       └── TEMPLATE.md
└── templates/
    └── ENTRY_TEMPLATE.md
```

- [`TIMELINE.md`](TIMELINE.md) is the chronological index.
- [`entries/`](entries/) contains individual journal entries grouped by year.
- [`reflections/weekly/`](reflections/weekly/) turns a week of separate signals into a wider reflection.
- [`templates/`](templates/) keeps the structure consistent without making the journal mechanical.

## Entry principles

1. **Preserve before interpreting.** Record the title, author, source, date, link, and available source text.
2. **Name uncertainty.** If metadata or technical evidence is unavailable, say so.
3. **Separate claim from fact.** A source author's statement remains an attributed claim until independently verified.
4. **Begin with clarity.** Give a new reader the central idea and why it matters before moving into analysis.
5. **Keep the human in the frame.** Ask who gains agency, who carries risk, and what kind of future the idea makes possible.
6. **Use Danika's voice.** Personal does not mean private; the journal may hold curiosity, uncertainty, and judgment without publishing private exchanges.
7. **Explain it plainly.** Every completed entry includes an **ELI21** explanation and a short glossary when specialist language is unavoidable.
8. **Make it adaptable.** Preserve a clear narrative arc—hook, context, tension, questions, and closing reflection—so the entry can become an audio or visual story later.
9. **Write to the future.** Close with a dated letter that captures what 2026 could and could not yet see.

## Status vocabulary

- `captured` — the source and first observations are preserved
- `reflecting` — interpretation is still developing
- `revisit` — the entry has explicit questions or claims to check later
- `closed` — no further review is currently planned

## A note on evidence

DLJ entries are records of attention, not endorsements. Technical, scientific, political, or commercial claims belong to their original sources unless an entry explicitly cites independent evidence. Corrections and later understanding should be appended with dates rather than silently rewriting what 2026 believed.
